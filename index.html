09. Mathematical Intuition: Why Homogeneous Coordinates?

Understand why 4D projective space $(X, Y, Z, W)^T$ is essential for camera matrices and computer vision.

Mathematical Foundations
Interactive 4D Projective Ray to 3D Cartesian Projection
1. Homogeneous Vector Definition & Scale Invariance
$$\mathbf{P}_{euclidean} = \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \\ Z \end{bmatrix} \iff \tilde{\mathbf{P}}_{homo} = \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \\ Z \\ W \end{bmatrix} \equiv \begin{bmatrix} X/W \\ Y/W \\ Z/W \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. Linearizing Translation into Matrix Multiplication
$$\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{P}_{cam} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{R}_{3 \times 3} & \mathbf{t}_{3 \times 1} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{P}_{world} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
3. Unified Matrix Composition ($\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{K} [\mathbf{R} \mid \mathbf{t}]$)
$$\begin{bmatrix} u \cdot Z_c \\ v \cdot Z_c \\ Z_c \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{P}_{3 \times 4} \begin{bmatrix} X_w \\ Y_w \\ Z_w \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
Homogeneous Scale Factor $W$: 1.00
Homogeneous $X$: 1.20
Homogeneous $Y$: 0.80
Normalized Euclidean Point $(X/W, Y/W)$ (1.20, 0.80)
Technique Trade-offs: Homogeneous Coordinates vs 3D Euclidean Space

✓ Strengths (Homogeneous Space)

  • Converts non-linear vector addition ($\mathbf{R} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}$) into a single linear matrix multiplication.
  • Represents points at infinity ($W=0$) cleanly without division-by-zero crashes.

✕ Weaknesses (vs 3D Euclidean)

  • Adds an extra dimension to all vectors ($4 \times 1$ instead of $3 \times 1$).
  • Requires explicit projective normalization (dividing by $W$ or $Z$) to return to physical 3D space.
Key Intuition: Homogeneous coordinates embed $N$-dimensional space into $(N+1)$-dimensional projective space. This allows translation and perspective division to be expressed as linear matrix multiplications, making camera projection chains ($\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{K}[\mathbf{R} \mid \mathbf{t}]$) unified and GPU-accelerated.
