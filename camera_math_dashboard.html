Deep-Dive: E vs F Matrices, Usages & 8-Point Algorithm
1. Matrix Structures & Degrees of Freedom:
  • Essential Matrix ($\mathbf{E} = [\mathbf{t}]_\times \mathbf{R}$): Relates calibrated normalized rays $\mathbf{x}_R, \mathbf{x}_L$. Has 5 Degrees of Freedom (3 rotation, 2 translation directions up to scale). Singular values $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2, \sigma_3 = 0$.
  • Fundamental Matrix ($\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{K}_R^{-T} \mathbf{E} \mathbf{K}_L^{-1}$): Relates raw uncalibrated pixel points $\mathbf{p}_R, \mathbf{p}_L$. Has 7 Degrees of Freedom and rank 2 ($\det(\mathbf{F}) = 0$).
2. Practical Usages in Computer Vision:
  • 1D Stereo Match Reduction: Computes right scanline $l_R = \mathbf{F} \mathbf{p}_L$ to search for matches in $O(N)$ instead of $O(N^2)$.
  • SVD Camera Pose Recovery: SVD of $\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{V}^T$ yields relative rotation $\mathbf{R}$ and translation $\mathbf{t}$ between stereo pairs (used in Visual SLAM & Structure from Motion).
  • RANSAC Feature Outlier Filtering: Rejects false feature matches by checking epipolar error $\mathbf{p}_R^T \mathbf{F} \mathbf{p}_L < \epsilon$.
3. Hartley's Normalized 8-Point Algorithm:
  1. Point Normalization: Transform points $\tilde{\mathbf{p}} = \mathbf{T} \mathbf{p}$ so centroid is at origin and average distance is $\sqrt{2}$.
  2. Form Linear System $\mathbf{A} \mathbf{f} = \mathbf{0}$: Build $N \ge 8$ constraint equations from $u_i' u_i f_{11} + u_i' v_i f_{12} + \dots + f_{33} = 0$.
  3. SVD Solution & Rank-2 Enforcement: Solve SVD of $\mathbf{A}$, set smallest singular value of $\mathbf{F}$ to 0 ($\sigma_3 = 0$), and re-project to enforce $\det(\mathbf{F}) = 0$.
  4. Denormalization: Compute final fundamental matrix $\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{T}_R^T \mathbf{F}_{norm} \mathbf{T}_L$.
Technique Trade-offs: Epipolar Rectification vs Raw Depth Point Clouds

✓ Strengths (Epipolar Reduction)

  • Dramatically speeds up stereo matching by reducing 2D searches to 1D scanlines.
  • Fundamental matrix $\mathbf{F}$ works without knowing uncalibrated internal parameters.

✕ Weaknesses (vs Solid-State LiDAR)

  • Requires strict physical or software image rectification alignment.
  • Highly vulnerable to mechanical camera vibration or thermal rig flex.
Key Intuition: Given a point $p_L$ in the left camera view, its corresponding 3D point lies on a 3D ray. This ray projects into the right camera as a 1D line (the **Epipolar Line** $\mathbf{l}_R = \mathbf{F} \mathbf{p}_L$). Stereo block matching only needs to search along this line!

09. Mathematical Intuition: Why Homogeneous Coordinates?

Understand why 4D projective space $(X, Y, Z, W)^T$ is essential for camera matrices and computer vision.

Mathematical Foundations
Interactive 4D Projective Ray to 3D Cartesian Projection
1. Homogeneous Vector Definition & Scale Invariance
$$\mathbf{P}_{euclidean} = \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \\ Z \end{bmatrix} \iff \tilde{\mathbf{P}}_{homo} = \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \\ Z \\ W \end{bmatrix} \equiv \begin{bmatrix} X/W \\ Y/W \\ Z/W \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
2. Linearizing Translation into Matrix Multiplication

In Euclidean space, 3D rigid motion is non-linear because vector addition ($\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{t}$) cannot be written as matrix multiplication with a $3 \times 3$ matrix:

$$\mathbf{P}_{cam} = \mathbf{R} \mathbf{P}_{world} + \mathbf{t} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{P}_{cam} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{R}_{3 \times 3} & \mathbf{t}_{3 \times 1} \\ \mathbf{0}^T & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{P}_{world} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
3. Unified Matrix Composition ($\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{K} [\mathbf{R} \mid \mathbf{t}]$)

Homogeneous coordinates enable chaining rotation, translation, and intrinsic pinhole projection into a single unified matrix product:

$$\begin{bmatrix} u \cdot Z_c \\ v \cdot Z_c \\ Z_c \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{K}_{3 \times 4} \mathbf{T}_{4 \times 4} \begin{bmatrix} X_w \\ Y_w \\ Z_w \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{P}_{3 \times 4} \begin{bmatrix} X_w \\ Y_w \\ Z_w \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Technique Trade-offs: Homogeneous Space vs Cartesian Vectors

✓ Strengths (Homogeneous Form)

  • Linearizes translation & perspective projection into unified matrix products.
  • Naturally handles points at infinity ($W = 0$) and vanishing lines.

✕ Weaknesses (vs Direct Cartesian Range)

  • Requires final perspective division by scale factor $W$ ($Z_c$).
  • Division-by-zero singularities when points lie on optical center plane ($W \to 0$).
Key Intuition: Every homogeneous point $[X, Y, Z, W]^T$ represents an entire 3D line passing through the origin in 4D projective space. Division by $W$ intersects this line with the 3D hyperplane $W = 1$. When $W = 0$, the vector represents a direction or point at infinity!
